# Reviewer 1
Review Report for Sustainable Cities and Society Manuscript Number: SCSI-D-26-09591R1

## Overall Comment
1. General Assessment The authors have made substantial revisions and have satisfactorily addressed most of the concerns raised in the initial review. The revised manuscript provides a more coherent spatial allocation method, additional comparisons with official damage reports, improved sensitivity analyses, and clearer explanations of the engineering and mortality-risk assumptions. The distinction between modeled planning estimates and observed disaster impacts has also been strengthened. The revisions have improved the methodological transparency and overall credibility of the study. Nevertheless, several limitations concerning spatial allocation, population exposure assumptions, and the interpretation of health-risk estimates warrant further clarification before publication.

(1) The additional comparisons with official damage reports are useful. However, the primary allocation still shows substantial spatial disagreement (total-variation distance = 0.5845), while the shaking-based alternatives demonstrate closer agreement. The authors should briefly clarify why the distance-based allocation remains the preferred primary scenario despite these findings and ensure that its limitations are reflected in the conclusions.

## Comment 4
(4) The retrospective analysis indicates that the later reported housing-damage total is approximately 17.95 times the initial value. This substantial difference highlights the sensitivity of estimated cooling and electricity requirements to the timing and completeness of disaster reporting. The practical implications of this limitation should be emphasized when discussing the applicability of the framework to emergency resource planning.

Thank you for highlighting this practical implication. # Reviewer 3

## Overall Comment
I thank the Authors for their careful response and for the substantive changes introduced. The main concerns raised in the first round have been addressed satisfactorily. The boundary of the exposed population is now explicit in the Abstract and in Sections 1, 2.3 and 3.1; the assumption underlying Eq. 3 is explicitly stated, accompanied by a sensitivity analysis of the demographic allocation, and acknowledged as a limitation. The transferability of the parameters from Katz et al. (2026) is discussed with comparative literature and distinguished from statistical uncertainty. The description of the engineering parameters has been expanded, with reference to Table S1, and the title “Spatial Screening Framework” is consistent with the outputs actually produced.
I particularly appreciate the replacement of the central surface with a coherent scenario, clearly distinguished from the median-based sensitivity analysis, and the presentation of comparisons with official reports as descriptive evidence without treating them as independent validation. I consider the revision satisfactory. The following two suggestions are optional and are intended solely to improve clarity; they are not conditions for publication.

## Comment 1
o1 – Interpretation of the primary allocation:
Section 3.3 shows that, in the comparison with the September 29 reports, the primary allocation yields a rank correlation of 0.7252 and a top-five overlap of 3/5, compared with 0.7732–0.8127 and 4/5 for the shaking-based alternatives. This difference is appropriately discussed in Section 4.5. The Authors may wish to briefly reiterate in the Conclusions that the primary specification serves as a reference for preliminary screening, without implying greater geographic accuracy than the alternatives tested, and that local priorities require field verification.

o2 – Temporal reference of the estimated quantities:
